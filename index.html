01 — Doctrine

The Synchronicity of Tech & Soil

Most digital commerce is built on hype half-lives — a product trends, converts, and depreciates inside a single quarter. HANDS & HEAD spent years inside that cycle. AGRO is the deliberate exit from it.

An avocado tree does not care about your ad spend. It takes four to five years to reach yield maturity, and it does not skip a season because the algorithm changed. That is the point. We are not optimizing for a traffic spike — we are compounding a physical asset that gets more valuable, more scarce, and more defensible every year it's in the ground.

What technology contributes is not hype — it's removal of friction. Automated pipelines (Make.com orchestration, Liquid-driven storefront logic, edge-delivered inventory state) collapse the distance between harvest and buyer to near zero. No brokers. No spoilage sitting in a middleman's warehouse. No CMS team. Just soil, root systems, and code doing exactly the parts humans shouldn't have to.

The soil compounds slowly. The infrastructure compounds instantly. AGRO exists at the intersection of both timescales.

03 — Architecture

Harvest to Buyer.
No Layer Between.

/infrastructure

Zero Monthly Cost

Static edge delivery, serverless order capture, no recurring platform rent. The margin stays with the soil, not a SaaS subscription.

/pipeline

Autonomous Routing

Order events trigger Make.com workflows that route allocation, update inventory state, and notify logistics — without a human in the loop until the crate ships.

/distribution

Direct Channel

B2B export lanes and D2C allocation share one backend. Same harvest, two demand channels, no broker taking a cut in between.

$ harvest.event → make.route(order) → edge.deliver(confirmation) → logistics.dispatch()
No CMS. No middleman markup. No idle inventory sitting in a warehouse ledger.
